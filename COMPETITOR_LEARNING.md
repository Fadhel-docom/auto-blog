# Competitive Learning System

This project should continuously learn from successful home-organization publishers without copying their branding, assets, or wording.

## Current lessons

### Editorial design
- Use a calm editorial hierarchy rather than a generic blog template.
- Make the homepage immediately explain the value of the site.
- Use strong featured content, clear categories, generous whitespace, and real-room photography.
- Keep navigation simple and make search/discovery obvious.
- Article pages should prioritize readability, useful headings, strong hero imagery, and clear visual rhythm.

### Content
- Start from a concrete reader problem, not a generic keyword.
- Prefer specific constraints: room, dimensions, renter limitations, awkward spaces, budget, or a clear before/after outcome.
- Use practical measurements, examples, mistakes to avoid, and actions the reader can actually take.
- Avoid filler paragraphs that merely repeat the keyword or heading.
- Build topic clusters and connect related articles internally.

### Images
- Use 6 strong, distinct images rather than flooding an article with 10+ images.
- Hero image: broad editorial scene representing the article.
- Section images: concrete, photographable storage objects and techniques.
- Avoid generic "organized home" searches when a specific room/object/solution can be shown.
- Prevent duplicate images and repeated scenes.
- Image selection should follow the final article content, not just the title.

### Traffic and distribution
- Pinterest should be treated as a visual search/discovery channel, not just a social feed.
- Test multiple useful angles from one article: problem/solution, before/after, checklist, seasonal angle, and specific room angle.
- Seasonal organization content should be prepared before the seasonal demand peak.
- Telegram/RSS provides automated distribution; performance must be measured with real clicks.
- Do not call a traffic source successful until analytics show it.

## Operating rule

**Observe -> understand why it works -> adapt -> automate -> measure -> improve.**

The project remains approximately 90% automated, with verification before consequential external actions.
