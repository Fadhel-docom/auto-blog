When a bathroom has no linen closet, it is tempting to add shelves until every spare towel and toiletry has a place. A small room then becomes harder to use: storage blocks the door, crowds the sink and leaves damp towels nowhere to dry.

This guide separates what must be within reach from what only needs to be nearby, shows how to measure shelf spacing from your own folded towels and explains why medicines belong somewhere other than the bathroom cabinet.

**Quick answer:** Keep daily toiletries and towels in use in the bathroom, store fully dry spare towels in a nearby dry place, keep bulk refills outside the room and move medicines to a cool, dry spot chosen by the label.

## Do not store the whole household in the bathroom

Separate three questions: what do you reach for every morning and evening, what only needs to be nearby, and what is backup? Daily products belong close to the sink or shower. Clean spare towels can live in a dry bedroom wardrobe or a hall cupboard if the bathroom is humid or crowded. Bulk refills do not need to be in the room at all.

## Create three clear storage zones

- **Daily-use zone:** products used every morning and evening in a shallow tray, drawer divider, mirrored cabinet or compact shelf, grouped by routine.
- **Clean-linen zone:** folded towels in a dry location. If the bathroom has a suitable wall or door, use hooks or a narrow shelf after checking door swing, wall material and clearance. A basket can hold a few towels if it stays away from repeated splashes.
- **Backup zone:** unopened toiletries, extra toilet paper and less-used supplies in a cupboard or lidded bin outside the bathroom when possible.

![Three panels: a daily-use zone in the bathroom, a dry clean-linen zone nearby and a backup zone outside the room](../images/bathroom-storage-without-linen-closet-zones.svg)

Homes & Gardens shows that baskets and flexible organizers can stand in for built-in storage; one suggestion is a basket near the toilet for spare rolls, or a decorative basket for towels elsewhere in the room.

## Count what the bathroom has to hold

Start from numbers, not from shelf shopping. For each person, count one bath towel in use and the next clean one, then add the hand towels you really use. A household of three with two hand towels comes to 3 x 2 + 2 = 8 towels in the room; everything above that can live elsewhere. For the full towel method, including door racks and a worked floor plan, see the towel storage guide linked below.

## Choose storage by the footprint you really have

A mirrored cabinet uses wall depth without taking floor space. A slim cart may fit beside a vanity, but measure the narrowest passage and check that it moves without catching the toilet, door or shower screen. Over-door organizers can help in rentals, but check door thickness, hinge clearance and whether the door still closes. Wall shelves use vertical space, but avoid spots that block ventilation, lighting or safe movement.

Before buying, measure width, depth, height, baseboard projection, door swing and the room needed to open drawers or doors.

## Measure shelf spacing from the folded towel

Fold the towels you own the way you will store them, then measure. In this example a folded bath towel is 12 inches (30 cm) wide, 10 inches (25 cm) deep and 3 inches (8 cm) high.

1. Stack three towels: 3 x 3 = **9 in** (23 cm).
2. Add 1 inch (2.5 cm) so you can slide the top towel out: 9 + 1 = **10 in** (25 cm) between shelves.
3. Check the shelf depth against 10 inches and the width against 12 inches per stack.

![Side view of a shelf holding three folded towels: 9 inches of stack plus 1 inch to lift gives 10 inches of spacing in an example](../images/bathroom-storage-without-linen-closet-shelf-spacing.svg)

Towel sizes vary, so these numbers are only an example. The method is the point: choose the shelf spacing from the folded stack, not the other way round.

## Keep towels and supplies dry

The U.S. EPA says the key to mold control is moisture control. In a small bathroom that means using the exhaust fan or opening a window when appropriate, drying wet items promptly and not packing damp towels into a closed cabinet or basket. Store only fully dry linens in closed storage, and let towels in use hang open to dry.

If a cabinet repeatedly smells musty or its contents feel damp, move linens elsewhere and address the moisture source before putting anything back.

## Spare rolls and cleaning supplies

Spare toilet rolls take surprisingly little space but are easy to scatter. A lidded basket near the toilet, or a narrow shelf that stays dry, can hold the few you need within reach; the rest belong in the backup zone. Cleaning products are a separate category: keep them in a closed container or cupboard secured away from children and pets, and do not store them with towels or medicines.

## Keep medicines out of damp storage

Do not assume the bathroom cabinet is a medicine cabinet. MedlinePlus advises storing medicines in a cool, dry place, such as a dresser drawer or a kitchen cabinet away from the stove, sink and hot appliances, because heat and moisture from showers and sinks may damage them. Keep medicines in their original container and out of reach and out of sight of children, follow any storage instructions on the label and ask a pharmacist if you are unsure.

![Three storage places compared: a bathroom cabinet where heat and moisture can damage medicines, and a bedroom drawer or kitchen cabinet away from the stove and sink](../images/bathroom-storage-without-linen-closet-medicines.svg)

## Check the room before you add anything

Stand in the doorway and walk through the room as if you were carrying a towel. If a new shelf, cart or basket narrows the route, blocks the swing of the door or covers a vent, it is the wrong storage even if it fits the wall. Also look at the floor: a basket on a damp floor next to a shower will collect splashes. Raise it, move it or choose a different spot.

## A simple no-linen-closet layout

For a compact bathroom, use:

1. A small daily tray or drawer divider at the sink.
2. Hooks for towels currently in use, with room to hang them open.
3. One measured shelf or basket for a limited number of fully dry spare towels, or none if the room stays humid.
4. A nearby dry cupboard or wardrobe for bulk refills and most extra linens.

This works because each category has a place and the bathroom holds only the quantity its space and ventilation can handle.

## FAQ

### Where should towels go without a linen closet?

Use a dry shelf, wardrobe, hall cupboard or a small bathroom shelf that stays dry and does not block movement. Keep only the quantity the room's space and ventilation can handle in the bathroom.

### Can towels go in a bathroom basket?

Yes, if they are fully dry and the basket is not in a persistently damp or splash-prone spot. Do not pack damp towels tightly into a basket or a closed cabinet.

### Can medicines stay in the bathroom?

Medicines are generally better stored in a cool, dry place such as a bedroom dresser drawer or a kitchen cabinet away from the stove and sink, because heat and moisture from showers can damage them. Always follow the label.

### How do I decide how much to keep in a small bathroom?

Count what you use daily and the next clean set, then keep only that near the sink and shower. Spare toilet rolls, bulk refills and extra linens can live in a dry cupboard elsewhere.

### How do I measure shelf spacing for folded towels?

Fold the towels you own the way you store them, stack the number the shelf will hold, measure the height, and add about an inch so you can lift them out.

### What if a bathroom cabinet smells musty?

Move linens elsewhere and address the moisture source before storing anything in it again, using ventilation and drying wet items promptly.
